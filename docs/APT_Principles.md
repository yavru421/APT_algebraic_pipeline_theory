# APT Principles (v2)

## 1. Universal Applicability Principle

APT can modularize and formalize any codebase, regardless of language or domain, into a reproducible algebraic pipeline.

## 2. Explicit Modularization

All workflows are decomposed into discrete, indexable modules (mi), each with explicit input/output variables and algebraic relationships.

## 3. Algebraic Notation

Pipelines are described as equations: y = mn(...m2(m1(x1, x2, ...))...), with all variables and dependencies explicit.

## 4. Compliance as a Module

Compliance (e.g., .gitignore, cleanup, archival) is treated as an explicit, indexable module in the pipeline.

## 5. Traceability and Reproducibility

All steps, variables, and results are documented, enabling full traceability and reproducibility.

## 6. Fork Differentiation Principle

APT-formatted forks are inherently unique and auditable, supporting reproducible research and collaborative development.

## 7. Meta-Pipeline Reflection

The process of APT-formatting itself is a meta-pipeline, enabling recursive improvement and automation.

## 8. Extensibility

New modules can be added by defining mk+1, Xk+1, Yk+1, fk+1, supporting future research and development.
